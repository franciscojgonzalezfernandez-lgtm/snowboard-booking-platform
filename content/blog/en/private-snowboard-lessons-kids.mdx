Parents ask me this every week, and my answer is honest rather than salesy: a private or small lesson is worth it for *most* kids, not all. It comes down to what actually helps a child learn — attention, and not being cold or bored.

## Why one-on-one works for children

A child in a class of eight spends most of the hour standing in a line waiting for a turn. A child with a coach watching only them is *riding* the whole time — and, just as important, is never left to get scared or freeze up alone. That's where the fast progress comes from, and where the tears don't.

Kids also copy. When I can ride right next to your child and show the exact move, small, at their speed, they mirror it far quicker than they'd follow an instruction called out to a group.

## Start at age 8

I take children from **age 8**. Younger than that and the strength and coordination to steer a board usually aren't there yet — the lesson becomes a struggle and the child leaves put off snowboarding rather than hooked. At eight and up, they pick it up fast and, more often than not, out-progress their parents.

## How long, and what it costs

For a child's first time I usually point parents at the shorter lessons (Season 2026/27):

- **Two hours — CHF 200** — my usual recommendation for a first real session.

The price is flat for up to four riders, so two siblings of a similar level can share the lesson and the cost.

## What I build into a kids' lesson

- **Warm breaks.** A hot-chocolate stop at COLORS is part of the plan, not a distraction. A cold child learns nothing.
- **Games, not drills.** Balance and edge control taught as games land far better than instructions.
- **Wins they can feel.** First stand, first slide, first stop — each one celebrated so they leave proud.
- **Video for the parents.** I film the session and send it, so you see exactly what they did.
