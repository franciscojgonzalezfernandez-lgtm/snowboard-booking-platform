Choosing your instructor is what most decides how much you improve in a day. It's not about certificates on the wall — it's how they teach. Here's what I'd check.

## 1. They actually ride, and speak your language

A good coach still rides and can explain why the board does what it does, not just recite a script. And teaching in your language isn't a detail: understanding the correction on the spot saves you half a day. I teach in English, German and Spanish.

## 2. Concrete feedback, not clichés

"Bend your knees more" fixes nothing. Look for someone who watches your turns, spots **the one thing** holding you back, and works on it until it sticks. I film along the way and send the video to your phone — watching yourself skid corrects faster than being told you skid.

## 3. That they fit your format

The fewer people and the more even the level, the more you progress. I teach **private** lessons of 1 to 4 at the same level (you decide who you bring) and also larger **group** lessons for friends or companies, when you want fun while you learn. What matters when choosing an instructor is that they give you real attention in either one and group by level.

## And how much does price matter?

It matters, but it's not first. One lesson that repairs your base turn is worth more than three cheap ones that never touch the root. You can see my durations and prices on [pricing](/en/pricing); if you're a larger group or a company, I'll put together a quote via [contact](/en/contact).
