"Small group" can mean very different things depending on who you ask. In a big class it can mean ten people and one instructor shouting from the front. Here it means something specific and unmovable: **one to four riders, and that's the ceiling.**

## Four is a limit, not a target

I cap every lesson at four because past that the teaching stops working. With four riders I can watch each of you take a run, name the one thing holding you back, and get to the next person before the first has cooled off. Add a fifth and someone is always standing still. So the number never creeps — four is the wall.

Most lessons are smaller than that anyway. A couple, a parent and one kid, two friends: those are the everyday shapes, and they're the ones where progress is fastest.

## Same level, mixed levels, or a big gap

Same-level groups share the most class: four riders who all link turns can chase one line all morning, and that's where a group flies. But **mixed levels work too** — it's just a bit more juggling. When a correction helps everyone I bring you together; when it doesn't, each of you works on your own thing while I rotate my attention.

If the gap is really wide — a first-timer alongside someone carving reds — I'll say so up front and **recommend** booking two separate lessons so nobody's bored or nobody's scared. It's a recommendation, not a rule: if you'd rather ride together, we make it work.

## What the small size actually buys you

- **No queue.** Four riders means four turns come round fast — you're riding, not watching.
- **Real feedback, by name.** I know which of you drops the back shoulder and which rushes the edge change.
- **Video for everyone.** I film through the session and send each rider their clips afterwards; you fix faster when you can see it.
