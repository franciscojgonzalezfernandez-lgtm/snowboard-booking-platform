People often frame this as a rivalry. It isn't. Both formats teach people to snowboard, and for some riders a large class is genuinely the right call. What follows isn't a knock on anyone — it's simply what changes when the group is small and the coach is yours.

## What a private lesson actually changes

The single variable that matters most in learning is **how much of the coach's attention lands on you.** In a large class, one instructor is divided across many riders and a fixed curriculum; you move at the group's pace, and a good chunk of the session is spent waiting your turn.

In a private lesson that variable flips. It's one coach and **one to four riders**, so:

- **There's no queue.** Turns come round in seconds, not minutes — you're riding, not standing.
- **The plan is yours.** I read how *you* ride and fix the one thing holding *you* back, rather than working through a set list that may not be your problem.
- **The pace bends to you.** Nervous? We slow down. Flying? We push. Nobody is held back and nobody is dragged along.

## The money, honestly

A private lesson sounds like the expensive option, and per person it can be — but often it isn't, because the price here is **flat for up to four riders** (Season 2026/27: CHF 110 for an hour, CHF 200 for two, CHF 385 for four, CHF 500 for a full day). A couple or four friends of a similar level split one price and each still gets individual coaching. Run the maths for your group before you assume a class is cheaper.

## When a big class is the better choice

I'd rather be straight with you than win every booking:

- You specifically want a **week-long group programme with the same peers** each day.
- You're on a tight budget riding **solo** and don't mind a set pace.
- Your child thrives in a **bigger group of kids** their own age.

If that's you, a large school may fit better — and that's fine.

## When to pick a private instructor

- You want the **fastest possible progress** in the time you have.
- You have a **specific goal** — first turns, carving, park, riding switch.
- You're a **family or group of mixed confidence** who want the session shaped around you.
- You'd rather learn in **English, German or Spanish**, one-to-one.
