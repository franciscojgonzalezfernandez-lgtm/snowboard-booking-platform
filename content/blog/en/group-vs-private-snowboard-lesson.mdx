It's the first choice you make when booking, and it changes the whole experience. Here's the real difference — and why I work the way I do.

## What each one is

A typical ski-school **group lesson** puts several people — often strangers — with one instructor. It's cheaper per head and has a social side, but the group sets the pace: if someone's slower, you wait; if you're slower, you rush. Attention per person is thin.

A **private lesson** is just for you, or the group you book with (1 to 4 people). The instructor watches your turns, picks the one thing holding you back, and works on it with you. You progress faster because every minute is yours.

## Why I teach private only

I teach groups of 1 to 4 at the same level, and you decide who you bring. No friction: no strangers at another level, and the lesson is built around you. I film along the way and the video lands on your phone after — so you see what you did and why it improved.

I group by level on purpose. An even group learns faster and gets less frustrated; if a few of you come with very different levels, I'll tell you straight — you'll get more out of two separate slots.

## When a group lesson makes sense

I'll be honest: if you want the cheapest option and don't mind slow progress, a big-school group lesson does the job. If you actually want to improve — or enjoy the day without waiting — private wins clearly, and in a group of friends the per-person price gets close.
